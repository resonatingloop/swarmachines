01 the premise

a tiny world.
a surprisingly complicated we.

a morphology-discovery engine for multi-agent systems: a way to see the forms that emerge as agents coordinate, disagree, fail, and recover.

the proposed world: freight in the fog.

four dispatcher agents route supplies between depots. each sees a different slice of the network: a queue, a delivery request, a bridge that just closed.

they exchange reports and choose actions. then we remove a communication relay or introduce an outdated route report. who gets heard? who becomes indispensable? what happens when that arrangement breaks?

the delivery map and the communication network are separate. we can change who talks to whom while keeping the underlying job comparable.

observe→exchange→act→record
what forms of relation make a swarm more capable without erasing distinction, provenance, dissent, or recoverability?
02 the inquiry

give the patterns names.
make them earn those names.

start with a few candidate forms and the events that would make them visible. leave room for mixed, unfamiliar, and uneventful runs.

specimen a / concentration

the little monarchy

one agent becomes the center of coordination, even when direct peer communication is available.

look for: concentrated message traffic, repeated deferrals, and what happens when that agent disappears.

specimen b / overlooked knowledge

the quiet specialist

one agent has useful local information, but the group’s plan fails to reflect it.

look for: a relevant report that was delivered and included in context, followed by actions that contradict it.

specimen c / redistribution

the repair cascade

a disruption prompts new exchanges and a workable redistribution of responsibility.

look for: changed communication patterns alongside resumed deliveries and a measurable recovery interval.

the demo gives us specimens. repeated, comparable runs tell us whether they recur. message volume alone doesn’t establish influence; these are behavioral hypotheses, not mind-reading.

03 the humans

four ways into
the same creature.

each lane owns something the project needs. choose by curiosity and experience; the boundaries are seams for collaboration.

01 / the worldbuilder

own the graph, local observations, legal actions, scoring, and disruptions. start with scripted dispatchers; pair with the nervous-system owner to swap in one model-driven agent. a substantial cs lane with a gentle agentic boundary.

02 / the nervous system

own private agent context, message delivery, adaption calls, validation, and langfuse traces. start with one complete observation-to-action loop, then connect all four agents.

03 / the observatory

own the visual world, communication overlay, timeline, and replay. start from a clearly labeled sample event log while the real runner takes shape.

04 / the field naturalist

own controlled scenarios, repeated comparisons, evidence extraction, and the research story. start with two disturbances and observable signs of recovery or failure.

the learning-loop branch

could better local coordination skills produce a different collective? curate examples from failures, adapt an agent, and compare on held-out scenarios. keep scenario generation separate from measured outcomes.

run→inspect→curate→adapt→compare

training is an optional branch. its cost, turnaround, deployment route, and a fair comparison against the base model need checking before it becomes part of the demo.

small enough to finish.

the core specimen

one world · four agents · two communication arrangements · two disturbances · structured events · replay · a few behavioral measures.
